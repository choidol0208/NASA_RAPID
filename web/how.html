How the score works

This page, the catalog, the ranking code and the data pipeline were written with an AI assistant (Claude) under the team's direction. Text written by a language model carries an "AI-generated" label that says whether a person has checked it against its source. The repository lists every AI use and every source.

Read the method: dimensions, weights, gates and evidence quality

Each of the 41 test configurations in the team's catalog is compared with your input on up to seven dimensions: oxygen, pressure, flow, fuel, sample shape, research purpose and, for suppression questions, the diluent gas. Each comparison gives a similarity from 0 to 1, shown as the strip on each result.

Weights depend on the kind of question (prevention, detection or suppression). They are derived by the analytic hierarchy process (AHP) from pairwise comparisons of the dimensions, each with a cited source: combustion textbooks, papers and NASA records; for suppression they are also pooled with how much each condition mattered in NASA's FLEX measurements. Flow matters because a microgravity flame has no buoyancy and gets its oxygen only from forced flow. A wrong fuel phase or a far-off oxygen level cannot be made up by other matches. The total is then scaled by evidence quality (raw data, flight duration, human check). How the weights were set, with sources

    The weights and the other scoring values are the team's proposal, not NASA guidance. The pairwise judgements were filled in by an AI session from the cited sources; a team member approved each step of the derivation, and the other scoring values after an AI review of each value, as they are.

    The same code ranks every query. A language model never produces or changes a score. Catalog values are extracted from the PSI records and tables and are marked when not yet checked by a person.
