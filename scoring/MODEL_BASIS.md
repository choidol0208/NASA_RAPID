# What the scoring rules rest on

*AI-generated (Claude Code, catalog role), 2026-11-14. Not reviewed by a combustion specialist.*

The catalog filter (`scoring/scorer.py`, numbers in `scoring/scoring_config.json`) is the team's ranking method, not NASA's conclusion. This note checks each rule against one combustion textbook:

> Turns, S. R. *An Introduction to Combustion: Concepts and Applications*, 3rd ed. McGraw-Hill, 2012. ISBN 978-0-07-338019-3.

How the textbook was used:
- It was read as background, to check the direction and the functional form of each rule.
- No text, figure, table or number from it is reproduced here or in the product.
- Section names are given so a reader can check the point.
- The textbook covers gas flames, droplets and carbon particles. It does not cover flame spread over solid materials, so the rules for solids rest on the PSI records and the investigators' papers instead.

| Oxygen similarity, decaying faster above the tested maximum (`similarity.o2`) | Oxygen in the oxidizer has a strong, nonlinear effect on diffusion flames: through the stoichiometric ratio, flame length scales with the inverse of the oxidizer's O2 mole fraction (Ch. 9, "Factors Affecting Stoichiometry"). A droplet's burning-rate constant grows with the transfer number, which rises with the heat released per unit of ambient oxidizer (Ch. 10, "Burning Rate Constant and Droplet Lifetimes"). So oxygen above the tested range means more intense burning than tested: the asymmetry has the right sign. | The two decay scales: ln(26/21) above and ln(21/11) below, chosen so that near air they equal the earlier 5 and 10 percentage points. | Direction supported. | Pressure similarity on a log scale, symmetric, σ = ln 2 (`similarity.pressure`) | Pressure enters through power laws. Laminar flame speed scales as a power of pressure that depends on the overall reaction order, and hydrocarbons show a weak negative dependence (Ch. 8, "Factors Influencing Flame Velocity and Thickness: Pressure"). A distance on log pressure matches power-law behaviour, and since the sign of the effect differs between quantities, a symmetric distance is defensible. | σ = ln 2. | Form supported. |

## Changes made from this check (config 1.1.0, applied 2026-11-14 at the human lead's request)

- **A. Oxygen distance on the log of the mole-fraction ratio.** Before, 15→21% and 30→36% were the same distance (6 points), although the first is a larger relative change and matters more for flame structure (Ch. 9).
  - Near air the scales equal the earlier ones.
  - Effect on the demo: `ISS` and `lunar habitat` keep their top 3; scores move by less than 2 points.
  - `ISS cotton fabric` swaps ranks 2 and 3.
  - `lunar habitat cotton fabric` and `달 기지 면직물` now rank SAFFIRE-I second, where BASS-II-C05 was.
- **B. Diluent similarity by molar heat capacity.** CO2 has a higher molar heat capacity than N2, and helium a lower one. Helium's high thermal diffusivity is a further effect, which this rule does not capture. Only suppression queries that name a diluent change; no top 3 of the validation scenarios changed.
